Information Security Principles

Information security is the practice of protecting information by mitigating risks. It involves the prevention or reduction of the probability of unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction of information.

Key Principles (CIA Triad)

  • Confidentiality — Ensuring information is accessible only to those authorized to access it. This includes client data, internal documents, and system credentials.
  • Integrity — Safeguarding the accuracy and completeness of information and processing methods. Data must not be altered by unauthorized parties.
  • Availability — Ensuring authorized users have access to information and associated assets when required.

Common Threats

  • Phishing attacks targeting employee credentials
  • Social engineering to bypass security protocols
  • Malware and ransomware infections
  • Insider threats from negligent or malicious employees
  • Unpatched software vulnerabilities

Your Responsibilities

As an employee, you are the first line of defense. Always verify email senders, never share passwords, and report suspicious activity immediately to the security team.

Data Privacy Regulations

Working in the insurance industry requires strict compliance with data privacy regulations including HIPAA, GLBA, and state-specific privacy laws.

Client Data Handling Rules

  • Never store client data on local drives or personal devices
  • Client data must only be stored in approved, encrypted repositories
  • Do not share client information via personal email or messaging apps
  • Hard-delete sensitive documents when no longer needed
  • Always use secure, approved channels for data transfer

Personally Identifiable Information (PII)

PII includes any data that could identify a specific individual: name, SSN, date of birth, address, phone numbers, email addresses, policy numbers, and claim details.

Breach Notification

If you suspect a data breach or unauthorized access to client information, you must immediately notify your supervisor and the security team. Document what happened, when, and what data may have been affected.

Workstation Security Policies

Your workstation is a critical access point to client systems. Maintaining its security is essential to protecting client data and company assets.

Clean Desk Policy

  • Lock your workstation (Win+L) whenever you step away
  • Keep desks clear of sensitive documents when unattended
  • Secure drawers with keys when not at your desk
  • Display boards must not contain confidential information

Software & Device Rules

  • Only approved software from BIC/corporate sites may be installed
  • USB devices are prohibited except for approved dongles
  • Do not connect personal devices to your work laptop
  • Install updates promptly and reboot weekly
  • No personal browsing, streaming, or social media on work devices

Authentication

  • MFA/2FA must never be bypassed
  • Never store passwords in browsers
  • Use the corporate password manager for credential storage
  • Change passwords immediately if compromise is suspected

Professional Communication Standards

Maintaining professional conduct in all communications reflects on the organization and ensures compliance with client requirements.

Email Usage

  • Use corporate email only for business purposes
  • Never forward work materials to personal email accounts
  • Do not send communications between internal and client systems unless authorized
  • Verify recipients before sending sensitive information

Meeting Etiquette

  • Turn on video during client meetings when video is expected
  • Maintain professional attire and a clean, well-lit background
  • Join from a quiet, distraction-free environment
  • Show eagerness to learn and contribute to discussions

Leave & Availability

  • Follow client-specific working hours
  • Communicate leave plans well in advance
  • Block calendars and set out-of-office messages on all platforms
  • Provide daily status updates for assigned stories

Regulatory Compliance

The insurance industry is heavily regulated. Non-compliance can result in significant fines, legal action, and reputational damage.

Key Regulations

  • HIPAA — Protects health information in health insurance contexts
  • GLBA — Requires financial institutions to explain information-sharing practices
  • SOC 2 — Defines criteria for managing customer data (security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, privacy)
  • State Regulations — Various state-specific insurance data protection laws

Incident Reporting Procedure

  1. Identify and document the incident immediately
  2. Notify your direct supervisor within 30 minutes
  3. Contact the security/compliance team via the designated channel
  4. Preserve any evidence (do not delete logs or messages)
  5. Cooperate fully with the investigation team
  6. Follow up on corrective actions assigned to you

Consequences of Non-Compliance

Violations of security and compliance policies may result in removal from the account, disciplinary action, or termination of employment. Both client and corporate organizations have robust threat detection systems in place.
